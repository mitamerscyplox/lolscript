League of Legends HWID ban explained

A League of Legends HWID ban blocks your computer, not just your account. This guide explains how Riot hardware bans work, how long a LoL HWID ban lasts, and what your options are.

The basics

What is a HWID ban in League of Legends?

HWID stands for hardware ID. Your PC exposes identifiers such as motherboard, disk, network adapter (MAC), and TPM serial numbers. A hardware ban links those identifiers to a ban, so the restriction follows the computer instead of a single Riot account.

Riot's Vanguard anti-cheat, which came to League of Legends in 2024, runs at kernel level and can read these identifiers. That is why a Riot hardware ban can also block new accounts created on the same PC.

Comparison

HWID ban vs account ban

Account ban

Applies to one Riot account. You can usually still log in with a different account on the same computer.

HWID ban

Applies to the hardware. New accounts created on the same PC can be blocked as well, because the ban is tied to your hardware identifiers.

How long it lasts

Riot does not publish a fixed length for hardware bans. Players report both temporary and permanent ones, and hardware bans are generally tied to serious violations such as cheating.

How to tell

If fresh accounts on the same PC are also restricted while other PCs work, the restriction is most likely tied to your hardware rather than to one account.

Your options

What you can do after a LoL HWID ban

  1. Appeal if you think it is a mistake

    Submit a ticket to Riot Support. Riot is the only party that can review or lift a ban on its side.

  2. Wait out a temporary restriction

    If Riot tells you the restriction is temporary, the only official option is to wait until it expires.

Riot does not publish a fixed duration for hardware bans. Players report both temporary and permanent hardware bans. Riot Support is the only reliable source for the status of your own ban.

Can I create a new account after a HWID ban?

A new account on the same PC can be blocked as well, because the ban is tied to your hardware identifiers rather than to one account.
